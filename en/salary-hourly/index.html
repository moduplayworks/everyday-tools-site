Calculation method and scope

Convert gross pay by pay frequency using weekly hours, workdays, and paid weeks to calculate annual and hourly equivalents.

Annual gross = entered pay × annual periods for that frequency. Hourly equivalent divides annual gross by weekly hours × paid weeks; workday equivalent divides by workdays per week × paid weeks.

Worked example

With 25 per hour, 40 hours per week, and 52 paid weeks, annual gross is 25×40×52=52,000 and the hourly equivalent is 25. The monthly equivalent is 52,000÷12≈4,333.33.

Pay frequencies

Pay for 2 workweeks is annualized from paid weeks. Pay twice monthly (2 payments each month) uses 24 periods in 1 year, monthly pay uses 12, and weekly pay uses paid weeks.

These are gross-pay equivalents in the entered currency. Taxes, insurance, overtime, bonuses, allowances, holiday pay, and legal interpretation of an employment contract are excluded. Results do not guarantee actual pay or payment dates.

Frequently asked questions

How does pay every 2 weeks differ from 2 payments per month?

Pay for every 2 workweeks uses half the paid weeks as its annual period count: 26 periods for 52 weeks. Twice-monthly pay (2 times per month) uses 24 periods per year, so the amount per period differs for the same annual gross.

Why enter weekly work hours and paid weeks?

The calculator divides annual gross by annual work hours to estimate an hourly equivalent. If you have unpaid leave, enter a paid-weeks estimate that reflects it; the hourly result will change.

Does this calculate take-home pay after tax?

No. It converts gross pay into equivalent amounts in the same currency. Use the separate Korea take-home pay calculator for estimates that apply Korean tax and insurance assumptions.

Calculation criteria and reference materials

  • U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — How the OEWS estimates are calculated

    BLS OEWS uses 2,080 hours per year (40 hours × 52 weeks) for many annual wage estimates. This calculator instead uses the work hours and paid weeks entered by the user.

  • U.S. Office of Personnel Management — Computing Hourly Rates of Pay Using the 2,087-Hour Divisor

    This source documents a 2,087-hour annual divisor for certain U.S. federal civilian pay calculations. It is an example of a specific federal pay rule, not a universal standard.
