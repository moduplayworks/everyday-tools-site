How dimensional weight is calculated

Compare one package's dimensional weight with its scale weight, and enter the divisor for the shipping service.

Dimensional weight = length × width × height ÷ volume divisor
Higher comparison weight (before carrier rounding) = max(scale weight, dimensional weight)

Worked example

A 40 × 30 × 50 cm package has a volume of 60,000 cm³. With a divisor of 5,000 cm³/kg, its dimensional weight is 12 kg. Compared with the 8 kg scale weight, the higher value before rounding is 12 kg. Actual billed weight and cost depend on each service's measuring, rounding, minimum and exception rules.

How to use

  1. Choose metric or inches and pounds.
  2. Enter the three outer dimensions of the packed box and its scale weight.
  3. Check the divisor for your shipping service, enter it and calculate.
  4. Review the pre-rounding comparison and check the carrier's current rates and billing rules.

Frequently asked questions

How is dimensional weight calculated?

Multiply length, width and height in the selected length unit, then divide by a divisor that matches the resulting volume and weight units. For example, cm and kg use a divisor in cm³/kg. This calculator handles one package at a time.

Which volume divisor should I enter?

Check the current value for the destination countries, carrier, service and rate type in the carrier's guide or quote. Official examples also differ: DHL lists 5,000 cm³/kg and 139 in³/lb in some guidance, while UPS US distinguishes 139 for Daily Rates and 166 for Retail Rates. No single value applies to every shipment.

Is this the final billed weight or shipping cost?

No. The result is the higher of dimensional weight calculated with your divisor and scale weight, before rounding. It does not include carrier dimension or weight rounding, minimums, size thresholds, package exceptions, contract rates, fuel or regional surcharges. Check the shipping service for the final billed weight and cost.

Can I calculate multiple packages or a pallet at once?

This tool calculates one package at a time. For a multi-package shipment, apply the measurement and billing rules to each package and check the carrier's aggregation method. This rectangular-box calculation does not cover pallets or irregular parcels.

Does a carrier divisor stay exactly the same after unit conversion?

Unit conversion preserves the calculation mathematically. The converted value can differ from a carrier's separately published rounded integer divisor, so enter the exact divisor for your service again after switching units.
