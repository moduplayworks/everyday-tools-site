Calculation method and scope

Estimate paint from a room's four-wall area after subtracting openings, using identical room count, coats and product-label coverage

Worked example

For a room 5 m long, 4 m wide and 2.5 m high, the four walls total 2 × (5 + 4) × 2.5 = 45 m². Subtracting 4 m² for doors and windows leaves 41 m²; two coats cover 82 m². At the editable example coverage of 10 m²/L, 82 ÷ 10 = 8.2 L. This coverage is an example, not a product recommendation.

Four-wall area for a rectangular room is 2 × (length + width) × wall height. Subtract doors and windows in each room, multiply by room count and coats, then divide by the paint coverage on the product label. Coverage varies by product, surface, color, tools and application method, so enter the label value.

The tool does not calculate ceilings, floors, door frames, skirting boards, sloped walls, surface texture or absorption, primer, extra coats for color changes or job losses. Check the product information and actual site conditions before buying.

Frequently asked questions

How is the amount of wall paint calculated?

For a rectangular room, calculate four-wall area as 2 × (length + width) × wall height, subtract door and window area, multiply by identical room count and coats, then divide by the product-label area-per-volume coverage. The calculator estimates walls only and excludes the ceiling.

Are 10 m²/L coverage and two coats recommendations?

No. 10 m²/L and two coats are editable starting examples. Actual coverage and coat count vary by paint, surface, application method and color change, so use the product label and instructions.

Can I calculate rooms of different sizes together?

One calculation is for multiple rooms with identical dimensions and opening area. Calculate rooms with different sizes or openings separately, then add their paint estimates.

Does this include ceilings or trim?

No. This calculator covers only the four walls of a rectangular room. It excludes ceilings, floors, trim, sloped walls, surface texture or absorption, primer, extra coats for color changes and job losses.

Calculation criteria and reference materials

  • Dulux — How much paint do you need?

    This guide calculates the area from wall height and width, subtracts doors and windows, accounts for coats, then divides by the m²/L shown on the paint tin or product information. It supports the general formula and input flow; it does not supply coverage for a specific product.

  • Benjamin Moore — Regal Select product specifications

    The product specifications list coverage and recommended coats for this specific paint. This tool does not reuse one brand's values as universal defaults; it asks for the label values of the product you choose.
