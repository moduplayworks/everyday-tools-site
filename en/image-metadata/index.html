EXIF and removal method

Input example

Select a JPG with GPS and camera details to inspect its photo metadata. Then choose Read embedded provenance claims to view the C2PA manifest title, tool and assertion entries. The page reports when it finds no readable manifest. It does not validate signatures or certificate trust, and these details do not prove that a depicted event happened.

EXIF can contain camera, viewing, orientation, GPS, etc. Other additional information such as XMP and IPTC is also checked, and the interpreted fields and additional information blocks of the file are separated and displayed. The list is limited to 200 items and 20,000 characters; all manufacturer-specific information is not interpreted.

The deletion copy converts the pixel displayed by the browser into a new PNG encoding, excluding non-video chunks such as EXIF, text, and profile data. Recheck the PNG chunk before saving and revalidate it with a camera parser. Chunks used for video representation, such as color space and gamma, may remain; the original ICC color may change. The size of the PNG copy may increase.

Frequently asked questions

Should I keep only position info while keeping the JPG format?

This tool re-encodes a photo as PNG to exclude shooting information and other non-video metadata. The original JPG remains unchanged, and the location and date text inscribed on the image pixels is not removed. The PNG size may increase.

Is it a safe photo if no information is displayed?

Indicates no interpreted items exist. Manufacturer-specific fields or broken information may be unreadable, so no guarantee that no information exists. A removed copy is provided after rechecking the output file, keeping only allowed video chunks.

Can the color or orientation differ between the original and copy?

Saving pixels in the direction shown by the browser without copying the original ICC profile. Colors may change due to color space conversion. Open a copy, check the direction, colors, and opacity, then use it. HEIC and WEBP moving PNG are not supported.

What are C2PA Content Credentials?

They are a standard for claims and related information about a file’s provenance and edit history. Claims are recorded by the file creator or a tool; they do not prove the truth, accuracy or context of an event.

Does a missing claim mean the photo was AI-generated?

No. It only means no readable C2PA manifest was found in this photo. Credentials may be absent or removed, and this tool does not detect AI-generated content.

Are the displayed signature issuer and time verified?

No. The tool displays values written in the manifest but does not check signature validity, certificate trust or revocation status. Do not treat the displayed name or time as a verification result.

Are the photo or C2PA details sent to a server?

The selected photo and manifest are processed only in this browser. The C2PA reader loads a module from this site after you press its button and does not request remote manifests. Filenames and claims are excluded from shared text and analytics events.

Does the metadata-free PNG keep Content Credentials?

The tool re-encodes the original pixels as a new PNG and does not copy the original C2PA manifest or camera metadata into it. The original file is unchanged.
