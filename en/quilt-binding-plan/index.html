Instructions and example

Required length=2×(width+height)+allowance. Joined length for n strips=n×usable length−(n−1)×join loss. Choose the smallest valid integer n.

For a 100×150 cm quilt, 20 cm allowance, 100 cm strips and 6 cm join loss, 6 strips give 570 cm. A 6 cm strip width requires 36 cm of fabric.

At most 10,000 straight-grain strips. Join loss must be below strip length. Pattern matching, bias cutting, shrinkage and retailer increments are excluded.

Frequently asked questions

Why are there fewer joins than strips?

Joining n strips into an open continuous strip creates n−1 joints. Include the final end closure in the separate finishing allowance.

Should I enter the finished binding width?

Enter the actual cut width before folding. Determine that width from the instructions for your chosen binding technique.
