Calculation criteria and usage method

Determining the exact, below, and above success probabilities for independent execution.

P(X=k)=C(n,k)pᵏ(1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ · the lower·upper·range is the sum of the corresponding probability mass · average=np, standard deviation=√(np(1−p))

Input example

When tossing a fair coin 10 times, the probability that the head appears exactly 5 times is 252/1,024=24.609375%. Less than 5 is 62.3046875%, and more than 5 is also 62.3046875%.

If n=10, p=0.5, and k=5, then the number of combinations C(10,5)=252, and the probability for each case is 1/1,024. Exactly 5 times is 252/1,024=24.609375%. Below 5 and above 5 is 62.3046875% each, and since both include 5, adding the two values exceeds 100%.

How to use

  1. Use the example value or enter numbers directly. The table format has one row per line.
  2. Click Verify result. Incorrect rows are marked and excluded.

Limitations and interpretation

n=0~10,000, p=0~1, k and the interval is 0~n integers. Two outcomes: success or failure. Independent trials with the same success probability each time. No normal approximation is used; extremely small probabilities are represented as log values to indicate limits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the success probability of 50% entered?

Enter 0.5 for p. n and k are integers with 0≤k≤n. If the end of the range is left empty, calculate up to n attempts; all specified ends are included.

When can binomial distribution be used?

Used when there are two results: success and failure, a fixed execution count, independent executions, and the same success probability each time. This calculation cannot be directly applied to successes or failures that affect each other or change over time.
