Calculation method and criteria

A tool for estimating EV charging energy, cost, and time from a battery charge-level range, your kWh rate, and average power, and for comparing charging scenarios.

Actual example

Charging a 60 kWh usable battery from 20% to 80% stores 36 kWh. At an entered 90% efficiency, the charger supplies 36 ÷ 0.90 = 40 kWh. At your entered rate of USD 0.20/kWh, the cost is USD 8.00 before any fixed fee; at 7.2 kW average input power, charging takes about 5.56 hours. These are example inputs, not current local rates or charging speeds for every vehicle.

Frequently asked questions

Does it look up current charging rates automatically?

No. Enter the electricity rate and currency code yourself. The IEA reports that EV charging costs vary with regional energy prices and that public fast charging can cost much more than home charging. This tool does not look up time-of-use rates, taxes, per-minute fees, parking, provider tariffs, or exchange rates.

How is charging time estimated?

Divide estimated charger energy by average input power across the selected charge range. Do not use a charger's or vehicle's maximum rating as the average. Actual time varies with charge curves, battery temperature, surrounding conditions, and charger sharing.

What should I enter for charging efficiency?

Enter the share of charger energy stored in the battery. If the vehicle or charger display shows only battery-side power, check the measurement basis of average input power so losses are not counted twice in the time estimate. The default 90% is an editable example, not a universal vehicle specification.

Does this guarantee the actual bill or vehicle charging curve?

No. This estimate uses only your kWh rate and fixed session fee. It does not calculate idle or per-minute fees, time-of-use rates, taxes, parking, vehicle-specific charge curves, or temperature effects. Check the current terms from the charging provider and vehicle maker.

Calculation criteria and reference materials

  • IEA — Global EV Outlook 2026: Electric vehicle charging

    Describes home-charging preferences and costs, regional electricity-price differences, public slow- and fast-charging markups, and limits from ultra-fast charging and temperature. This tool uses only your inputs; it does not apply IEA rates or vehicle-performance averages.

  • U.S. Department of Energy — Charging Electric Vehicles at Home

    Shows a charging-cost example based on EV battery capacity and a user-entered kWh rate, and notes that time-of-use residential tariffs exist. The U.S. example rate is not a current rate for other regions.
