Calculation method and criteria

Compare coupon order and checkout totals, then compare partial-return refunds with retained discount allocation or minimum-spend enforcement.

Actual example

Starting from 50,000 won, subtract 10% first and then 5,000 won, giving a product price of 40,000 won. The reverse order gives 40,500 won. If the discount results in 40,000 won or more and free shipping applies, both have a shipping fee of 0 won. If the free shipping threshold is changed to 45,000 won, both orders incur a shipping fee. The partial-return example uses an original eligible subtotal of KRW 120,000, a KRW 50,000 return, an actual discount of KRW 20,000 and a KRW 100,000 minimum spend. Rounding the returned goods' discount to KRW 8,333 gives a retained-allocation refund of KRW 41,667. The kept goods total KRW 70,000, below the minimum; withdrawing their KRW 11,667 discount gives a KRW 30,000 refund.

Frequently Asked Questions

For duplicate discounts, should the discount rate be added?

No. Each fixed-rate coupon is multiplied by the product price just before applying the coupon. For example, 10% twice results in a 19% discount instead of 20%. Order differences occur based on fixed-rate coupons, maximum discount limits, and minimum purchase thresholds.

Is this similar to the actual payment in the e-commerce store?

Calculations based on directly entered assumptions. Allowed multiple use of coupons·Products excluded from application·Card discount is not automatically checked. Minimum purchase threshold is the amount of the product at the beginning or just before application·Selects rounding to the nearest whole number·Rounding·Rounding also applied. Shipping fees do not include coupons

Does it automatically find the minimum payment combination?

It compares input order and reverse order with unused coupons and does not explore all permutations and coupon combinations. Up to 5 orders can be changed directly. Free shipping means no condition, and 0 always means free shipping. The maximum discount field is unlimited, and 0 means no discount.

Does this determine my actual partial-return refund?

No. It compares retaining the allocation of one entered coupon with enforcing its minimum spend. All goods are assumed eligible; taxes, shipping, fees, points, item exceptions and stacked coupons are excluded. Check the seller's order details and policy for the actual refund.

Is the deduction shortfall money I owe?

No. If the discount withdrawal exceeds the calculated refund, the payable refund is shown as zero and the difference is separated as a deduction shortfall. It does not establish a customer debt or an actual additional charge.
